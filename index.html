STS & Age Correction Calculator

Standard threshold shift check with optional OSHA age correction (29 CFR 1910.95, Appendix F).

Why check for a threshold shift?

Employees exposed to noise at or above an 8-hour average of 85 dBA must be in a hearing conservation program. That means a baseline audiogram and a new audiogram every year (29 CFR 1910.95). Comparing each annual audiogram with the baseline catches noise-induced hearing loss early, while there's still time to keep it from getting worse.

A standard threshold shift (STS) is what triggers action under OSHA. The employee must be told in writing, hearing protection must be refit and the employee retrained, and the employee may need a referral. Some STS cases also have to be recorded on the OSHA 300 log. Hearing can change with age, so OSHA lets you subtract an expected age-related allowance before deciding whether a shift is from noise.

OSHA vs. NIOSH

OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration) is the federal agency, part of the U.S. Department of Labor, that sets and enforces workplace safety and health standards. Its noise standard is law for covered employers.

NIOSH (National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health) is the federal research agency, part of the CDC in the Department of Health and Human Services, that studies work-related injury and illness and makes recommendations to prevent them. It is not a regulatory agency, so its recommendations aren't legally enforceable. For noise, NIOSH recommends a lower exposure limit than OSHA (85 dBA vs. 90 dBA over 8 hours).

Both agencies were created by the Occupational Safety and Health Act of 1970.

Step 1

STS Check

Compares the current audiogram with the baseline at 2000, 3000 and 4000 Hz in each ear. A standard threshold shift (STS) is an average change of 10 dB or more at those frequencies.

If either ear shows an STS, use Step 2 to see whether it remains after age correction.

NIOSH comparison adds 500, 1000 and 6000 Hz and flags a change of 15 dB or more at any single frequency (500–6000 Hz) in either ear. NIOSH requires the shift to be confirmed by a second test at the same frequency in the same ear, and does not use age correction.

Step 2

Age Correction

Age correction is optional under OSHA Appendix F. The aging allowance is the table value at the current age minus the value at the baseline age, from Table F-1 (male) or Table F-2 (female). It's subtracted from the current thresholds before the shift is recalculated.

Ages 20 and under use the "20 or younger" row; ages 60 and over use the "60 or older" row.
